# Pilot-band source probe 2 — 2026-09-25

The first probe (`docs/trl-probe-2026-09-24.md`) found no free source that
reaches the pilot band (TRL 5–8). The owner's ruling after the placement
check: "the problem is lack of content, we're even missing evidence that
might change a 1 into a 2 or 3." This probe measures seven free sources that
sit closer to pilots, approvals, purchases and launches than papers do,
across seven technologies. Script: `docs/experiments/trl/probe2.py`. Raw
responses: `data/trl/probe2/` (gitignored, not committed). No model calls.

**Windows.** Fast sources (vendor press rooms, regulations.gov, the Federal
Register, SBIR): trailing 8 weeks, 2026-07-31 to 2026-09-25. Slow sources
(FAA and DOT pages, CORDIS, exhibitor directories): trailing 12 months,
2025-09-25 to 2026-09-25. **The slow sources' counts are a stock, not a
flow**: they say what exists, not what arrived this quarter.

**Pass line (the brief's).** A cell (source × technology) passes with 3 or
more pilot-band documents in its window. A pilot-band document is one whose
title or first paragraph describes a pilot, first site, purchase, contract,
approval or scaled operation by a named actor. Counts are hand reads,
conservative, at most the first 10 per cell unless noted. A source passes if
3 or more of the 7 technologies pass.

## Run

```
SEC_CONTACT_EMAIL=<contact> python docs/experiments/trl/probe2.py
```

Every raw response is cached; a re-run reuses the saved file unless
`--refresh`. That matters for regulations.gov (below). Before the script,
newsroom URLs, feeds, sitemaps and robots.txt files were found by hand; the
vendor URLs in `VENDORS` are the ones that answered, and the ones that did
not are listed under source 6. The script calls `session.get` directly in two
places, to keep the body of a refusal and to save the binary CORDIS zip,
because `http.fetch` returns text and raises on a refusal.

**Query terms.** Each technology's watchlist include patterns, written out
as plain phrases (`VARIANTS` in the script): for example
`autonomous truck`, `autonomous trucks`, `autonomous trucking`,
`driverless truck(s)`, `self-driving truck(s)` for autonomous trucking, and
`drone delivery`, `delivery drone(s)`, `BVLOS` for delivery drones. Neither
regulations.gov nor the Federal Register stems a quoted phrase, so each form
is its own request.

**regulations.gov rate limit.** The brief expected about 25 requests an hour
on `DEMO_KEY`. The API's own header said `x-ratelimit-limit: 10`. The first
batch (one exploratory request, seven first-phrase queries, `autonomous
trucks`, `BVLOS`) used all ten; the eleventh (a test of `OR` syntax) was
refused `OVER_RATE_LIMIT` at 16:38 UTC. The final run at about 16:55 UTC
asked for the next form (`autonomous trucking`) and got 429; the script then
stopped asking. **Ten phrase forms (plurals and synonyms) were never run on
regulations.gov**; they are marked "not run: rate limit" in the table. The
Federal Register, which needs no key, ran every form and found the same
pattern.

| Federal Register | 0 of 7 | — | yes |

| SBIR.gov | 0 of 7 | — | API 403; HTML yes, index lags |

| Exhibitor directories | 0 of 7 | — | JS apps; Automate names only |
| EU Funding & Tenders portal | not measured | — | JS shell |

**No source passes for 3 of 7 technologies. The pilot band is still out of
reach of free, mineable sources**, with one exception: a vendor newsroom
can carry a technology whose vendors are public companies announcing
operations weekly (Kodiak in autonomous trucking). Across all sources and
windows the hand count found 7 pilot-band documents: 5 from press rooms
(autonomous trucking 3, delivery drones 1, electric trucks 1) and 2 from
CORDIS (electric trucks 1, digital product passport 1), none for
humanoid_logistics, gs1_2d or piece_picking.

**The research band** (documents that would give proposes or prototypes
claims): CORDIS is the only source with volume, and only for digital
product passports (28 projects signed or started in 12 months; 9 of the 10
read are research-band). Elsewhere it is 1 or 2 per technology (CORDIS
humanoid 1, delivery drones 2; SBIR humanoid 2 by 2025 year tag; press
humanoid 1) or zero. None of these sources will move a technology from 1 to
2 or 3 on its own, except CORDIS for digital product passports.

**The cap of 10.** The autonomous-trucking press cell had 14 documents in
window. Read strictly as the first 10 by date, it holds 2 pilot-band
documents (the Kodiak DMV permit of 08-14 is 12th) and does not pass. All
14 were read, and 3 is the count reported; the pass rests on that choice
and on one vendor.

## Recommendation, ranked by evidence per unit of collector effort

1. **Vendor press-room collector, per vendor, server-rendered or RSS only.**
   The only source with pilot-band hits in the 8 weeks (5 of 7 found).
   Effort: one small parser per newsroom and a dateline filter; breakage
   when a site is redesigned. Feeds **pilots, buys, sells, demonstrates,
   operates_at_scale**. Press releases are issued for redistribution. On
   this probe it clears the bar for autonomous trucking only; widening the
   vendor list per technology is the variable to test next, and Zipline,
   Flytrex and Tesla stay out until their newsrooms can be fetched.
2. **CORDIS bulk-CSV collector, monthly, joined to `organization.csv`.** One
   file, structured, open data; the join supplies named actors that the
   objectives leave out. Feeds **proposes, prototypes, demonstrates** and
   funded **pilots** (planned). Strong for digital product passports, thin
   for everything else, EU-only. Cheapest collector here.
3. **For everything else, the paid news licence stays the route** (spec §6,
   STATUS §7 item 6). Nothing free probed here replaces it for humanoid
   logistics, GS1 2D, piece picking, or for the pilot band generally. If a
   government watch is wanted for delivery drones and autonomous trucking,
   the Federal Register API (no key) with an agency vocabulary list
   ("automated driving systems", "package delivery", "unmanned aircraft",
   exemption notices) is the cheaper of the two dockets sources; it found
   no pilot-band document in this window.

## Rejected sources and what would reverse each

- **regulations.gov.** 0 pilot-band; delivery-drone hits are weekly lists
  and agricultural BVLOS exemptions; every other technology returns nothing
  on the watchlist phrases. *Reverse if* a registered api.data.gov key (the
  owner's to obtain) and an agency-vocabulary query set yield 3 or more
  named-operator exemption grants or approvals for one technology in an
  8-week window, for 3 technologies.
- **Federal Register.** 0 pilot-band in window. *Reverse if* the agency-
  vocabulary queries produce 3 or more named-operator exemption applications
  or grants per 8 weeks for 3 technologies; today it reaches only
  autonomous vehicles and drones.
- **FAA pages; NHTSA, FMCSA, USDOT, FHWA pages.** 403 at the edge. *Reverse
  if* the lists are published as fetchable files (data.gov or similar) or
  the pages answer an identified client.
- **SBIR.gov.** API 403; HTML index shows one 2026 award across seven
  queries; phrase search not honoured; date fields misaligned. *Reverse if*
  the API answers and the current fiscal year's awards are loaded within a
  quarter of award, then re-test Phase II awards naming a customer.
- **Exhibitor directories.** JavaScript apps, a robots-disallowed data
  endpoint, or names without products. *Reverse if* an organiser (MHI, A3,
  CTA) publishes its exhibitor list with product categories as a page or
  file that can be fetched; then "sells" becomes countable as a stock per
  show.
- **EU Funding & Tenders portal.** JavaScript shell; its project data is
  CORDIS's. *Reverse if* procurement (tenders and contract awards, "buys")
  is wanted, which is a different dataset not probed here.

## Concerns

- **The one pass is thin.** Three Kodiak releases carry it; the 10-document
  cap would fail it.
- **Vocabulary.** The zeros from regulations.gov and the Federal Register
  are partly the watchlist's words: agencies write "automated driving
  systems" and "battery electric", not "autonomous truck" and "electric
  truck". The side test shows the difference; a production query set would
  need its own vocabulary.
- **regulations.gov on DEMO_KEY is 10 requests an hour here**, not 25. Any
  collector needs a registered key.
- **CORDIS robots.txt** disallows `/data/` and `/search` to all agents. The
  CSV was downloaded once because it is the published distribution of an
  open dataset on data.europa.eu, not by crawling; the owner should confirm
  that reading. One exploratory request to the CORDIS search
  (`/search?format=json`) was made before robots.txt was read; it was not
  used.
- **Cloudflare on Automate** challenged `curl` and passed the script's
  client on the same pages minutes apart; a collector there would be
  unreliable.
- The press extractor's dates are heuristic (nearest date string; month
  only for Volvo's URL paths); every counted item was read by hand.
