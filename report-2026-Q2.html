How to read this document

This report tracks supply chain technologies moving from idea to experimentation, investment, deployment and diffusion — built entirely from public data. Nothing here is a survey or an opinion: every number is a count of documents that a stored pattern matched, and every count can be traced back to the document that produced it.

Collection runs weekly so that each query stays inside its source's result cap. Reporting is quarterly, because two thirds of technology-weeks hold nothing at all and a weekly ranking mostly reports which week a collector caught something.

What is in it:

  • In summary — the quarter in a few lines, for a reader who reads nothing else.
  • What the quarter held — the main table: every technology, its documents by kind of evidence, and how concentrated that evidence is in one kind.
  • Substance and attention — what is being built against what is being talked about.
  • Stage Board — where each technology sits in the pipeline, from idea through to diffusion.
  • Where the money went — federal awards, and where they were spent.
  • What moved — changes against the previous quarter, where the comparison is sound enough to report.
  • Appendices — every tracked technology, and which sources feed which stage.

What it will not do. A score is withheld whenever the period or its comparison window is incomplete, and share movement is withheld for a technology drawing almost all its evidence from a single source — because that measures the source's coverage, not the technology. Where something is withheld the report says so and says why.

Table explanation

Each family column gives the document count and, beside it, that count as a percentage of the family's whole supply-chain corpus for the quarter. 100% would mean every document that family retrieved mentioned the technology; nothing comes close, and those are the true magnitudes. A rate can also be compared across quarters, which a rank cannot: if every technology doubles, every rank stays where it was.

Read the small corpora with care. Filings and trade press are searched by phrase rather than swept, so their corpora are small — a single document moves them by percentage points, where research and code hold thousands.

Stage names the family supplying most of the evidence. A technology concentrated in one family is not a fault: it is a technology at that stage, and the concentration column says how strongly.

Counts are documents matched, not mentions: a technology named twice in one filing counts once. The columns are kinds of evidence rather than databases: research covers preprints and journals together, and other gathers trade press, regulation, federal awards, research funding and community discussion. The concentration column names the kind that supplied most of the evidence, which is usually most of it.

20 of 40 technologies — holding 326 of 711 documents — draw 80% or more of their evidence from one kind. That is reported rather than suppressed: a technology whose documents sit almost entirely in research is a technology at the research stage, and withholding its movement would delete the finding along with the risk. What the concentration column asks you to keep in mind is that one source's coverage can look like a technology's trajectory.

Diversity is counted across kinds of evidence rather than source names, because two sources measuring the same thing are one piece of evidence twice: research (preprints and journals), code, patents, filings, trade press, regulation, federal awards and community discussion. A technology is marked when one kind supplies 80% or more of its documents, or when fewer than two kinds reach 1 document.

Substance and attention

Documents that are building against documents that are talking

Figure explanation

Horizontal is attention — trade press and community discussion. Vertical is substance — code, patents, filings, federal awards and regulation. Research sits in neither: a preprint is not a built thing and it is not hype, and nine hundred of them would drown the distinction.

The dashed line is where the two are equal. Above it, more is being built than said; below it, more is being said than built.
